The 16 Pro is absent from this site because it was absent from the pages
recovered: it is a gap in the source, not a transcription loss.

Everything below comes from **Paillard's own sales brochure**, read in full.
That is a primary source — the manufacturer describing its own product — so
these are not claims needing a second voice.

**Source:** <https://www.pacificrimcamera.com/rl/00936/00936.pdf> — read
2026-11-18. Published by Paillard Inc., 1900 Lower Road, Linden, N.J., with
a Los Angeles address also given. Dating code `7/70` in the corner of the
back page, so July 1970 or thereabouts.

## What it is

A professional 16mm camera, electronically driven, shoulder-carried with a
400ft coaxial magazine at the rear. "A truly professional 16mm motion
picture camera designed for the modern style of moviemaking."

**Confidence: certain** — the brochure states all of it.

## The models, and what "16 Pro 100" means

This is the thing the name hides. The back page lists four:

| Sound | Speed range |
|---|---|
| Double system | 16–50 f.p.s. |
| Double system | **16–100 f.p.s.** |
| Single system | 16–50 f.p.s. |
| Single system | **16–100 f.p.s.** |

So **16 Pro 100 is the high-speed variant**, not a separate camera. The
body plate in the photographs reads `BOLEX 16 PRO 100`, and the lens cap
`BOLEX 16 PRO 100`.

Two order numbers are given for the versions: **#2510** for silent and
double-system sound, **#2500** for magnetic single system, double system
and silent.

## Specifications, as stated

- **Motor** — purpose-built Vario-Synchro, 3-phase induction, built in.
  Variable 16–50 f.p.s., or 16–100 on the Pro-100. Constant 24 and 25
  f.p.s. for sync sound. Runs forward and reverse. Single-frame for
  animation and time lapse. No brushes or collectors.
- **Shutter** — mirror, 132° opening, 1/60 sec at 24 f.p.s., mounted
  directly on the motor shaft.
- **Magazine** — 400ft coaxial, die-cast aluminium with a Macrolon lid,
  takes 100/200/400ft on reels or cores, Winding B. Two-chamber. Works
  forward or reverse. Two footage counters, one per chamber. Mounted at a
  slight angle to correct lateral displacement.
- **Threading** — automatic; ready to film in under 4 seconds from
  attaching the magazine, with no manual loop forming.
- **Transport** — single high-precision claw, no separate registration pin.
  Vee lateral guides. Steadiness "better than 0.1%" guaranteed. A film
  knife is built into the camera.
- **Exposure** — built-in photoresistor, through the lens, automatic
  diaphragm by servo motor, coupled to the speed control so exposure
  adjusts when speed changes mid-shot. 12–1600 ASA. Buzzer on insufficient
  light. Aperture visible in the finder and on the side of the body.
- **Temperature** — stated to operate from −4°F to +120°F.
- **Power** — 12V DC, inverted to 3-phase AC. Belt-worn battery pack and
  control unit, with charger and an optional synchronizing crystal.
- **Build** — most components are plug-in modules in an araldite sealing
  compound, meant to be exchanged by the user rather than sent for service.

## What we still need

- **Years.** The brochure is c. July 1970; it does not say when the model
  was introduced or discontinued. A catalogue or price list would settle
  it — Pacific Rim has 16mm price lists for Feb 1972, Oct 1976, Jan 1978
  and Nov 1979, which would at least bracket how long it was sold.
- **Lens mount.** The brochure never names it. The photographs show a large
  bayonet with an Angénieux 12-120mm f/2.2 zoom fitted.

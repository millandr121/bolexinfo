# Bolex Stereo (Triorama)

A 3-D attachment for the non-reflex H-series, not a camera. Recorded here
rather than published because the accessories section of the site is built
from the recovered original pages and has no route for a researched entry
yet; adding one needs a change to that section, not another data file.

## What it is

Single-strip 16mm 3-D, recorded and projected on ordinary film. Two rear
lenses sit 5.3 mm apart, and a prism block widens that to an interocular of
64 mm, close to the human average. f/2.8, fixed focus, set at the
hyperfocal distance for about 10 ft. On the projector side a 20 mm lens
with polarising filters at 135° and 45° separates the two images again, so
viewers need polarised glasses and a silver screen.

Sold from 1952, apparently to about 1954. US$397.50. Fits the non-reflex
Bolex only: the reflex prism in an H-16 Rex sits where the attachment's own
optics have to go.

The Triorama name appears on the projection side of the system in some
material, the Stereo name on the camera side. They are one product.

## Where this comes from

Hess, Hillary (2024). "Bolex Stereo." In James Layton (ed.), *Film Atlas*.
www.filmatlas.com/entry/150. Brussels: FIAF and Rochester, NY: George
Eastman Museum. Read in full, November 2026. No licence is stated on the
entry, so it is cited and not copied: the prose above is this archive's
own. Some images there carry separate credits.

Pacific Rim lists a "Bolex Sterero Instruction Book" (their spelling),
which would be the manufacturer's own account of it. Not yet read, and
worth reading before this is published.

## What is still unknown

- The exact end of production. 1954 is the Film Atlas reading.
- How many were made, and whether it sold at all at that price.
- Whether the projection lens was sold separately.

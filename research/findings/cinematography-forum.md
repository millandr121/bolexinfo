# The cinematography.com Bolex forum

**Status:** harvested, not yet drawn on for site content
**Source:** cinematography.com forum 78, via the Wayback Machine
**First harvested:** 2026-11-18

## What it is

cinematography.com ran a **forum dedicated to Bolex** — forum 78, 27 listing
pages, **571 threads spanning 2003 to 2026**. It is the single richest body
of Bolex knowledge found so far, and unlike a catalogue or a brochure it is
*argued*: people bring a broken camera, several specialists disagree, and
the disagreement is where the knowledge is.

The live site is shut to automated reading (see `../sources.md`). The
Wayback Machine holds it.

## Coverage, and what is missing

571 threads were listed on the archived index pages. Only some of those
threads were themselves captured:

- **326 recovered in full (57%)** — every post, with author and date
- **245 never archived (43%)** — the index page recording the thread
  survives, the thread does not

The misses are real, not a fetching bug: the Wayback Machine returns a
genuine 404 for them, and a date-wildcard replay that bypasses the CDX
index entirely finds nothing either. They are scattered across the whole
id range rather than clustered in one lost period, so there is no single
re-crawl that would recover them.

**The titles of the missing threads are kept anyway.** A thread that cannot
be read is still a recorded lead — someone with a forum login can open it,
and the index says it exists.

## How it may be used

These posts are the writing of named, living specialists. The rule set in
`../sources.md` applies: facts restated in our own words and cited; short
quotations with the author named; no bulk republication; nothing attributed
to anyone who did not write it.

The **search** idea (see `../roadmap.md`) is the strongest form this can
take, and also the most defensible one: a short quotation, the author's
name, the date, and a link back to the archived thread is what a citation
*is*. It credits rather than appropriates.

# Outreach

Drafts for asking people directly. Nothing here has been sent — these are
for Andrew to send, edit or discard.

The reasoning: the strongest position for this project is not a careful
reading of what we may take without asking. It is having asked. Most of
the people whose knowledge is being preserved here are alive, reachable,
and would probably be glad someone is doing it. A short honest note turns
a grey area into a collaboration, and sometimes into new material — people
who are asked tend to offer more than was requested.

Standing rule while nothing has been answered: **no permission is claimed
anywhere on the site.** Same as with Michael's work.

To Dom Jaeger, Simon Wyss, Jean-Louis Seguin and others whose posts would
be quoted. They can be reached through cinematography.com itself, or in
Jean-Louis Seguin's case through his repair business.

> Subject: Preserving the cinematography.com Bolex forum
>
> Hello,
>
> I run bolexcollector.org, which is keeping Michael Tisdale's
> BolexCollector.com online while the original is down.
>
> I've been looking at the Bolex forum on cinematography.com, and the
> reason I'm writing is that it's disappearing. The live site now blocks
> automated access, and of the 571 threads I can find listed, only 326
> still exist in the Internet Archive. The rest are gone — title only.
> Your posts are a large part of what's left: by my count you're among
> the most prolific contributors there, and the threads where you explain
> how these cameras actually work are the most useful Bolex material
> anywhere on the internet.
>
> I'd like to make that searchable, so someone with a sticking H16 can
> type their problem and find the answer you already gave. What I have in
> mind is short quotations, always with your name, the date, and a link
> back to the original thread — a citation, not a copy.
>
> I wanted to ask rather than just do it. Two questions:
>
> 1. Are you happy to be quoted that way?
> 2. Is there anything of yours you'd rather not see reproduced, or
>    anything you've since corrected and wouldn't want repeated?
>
> And if you have material of your own — notes, manuals, photographs —
> that you'd like kept somewhere permanent, I'd be glad to host it and
> credit it properly.
>
> Thanks either way, and thanks for everything you've already written.

## 4. Musée d'Yverdon et région (MYR)

The most valuable single ask on this list. They hold, and have already
published on notrehistoire.ch under "Tous droits réservés":

- `Usine Paillard S.A. (Yverdon)` — the camera assembly line
- `Bolex, atelier de réparation` ×3 — the repair shop
- `Paillard SA (Sainte-Croix)`

Nothing else found anywhere shows Bolex cameras actually being built or
repaired. They are a public museum, they chose to publish these openly,
and the request is non-commercial heritage use, so this is a far better
prospect than a company.

Reachable through notrehistoire.ch or the museum directly.

> Subject: Using a few of your Bolex photos
>
> Bonjour,
>
> I look after bolexcollector.org, a small preservation site for
> Paillard-Bolex cameras. It keeps Michael Tisdale's BolexCollector.com
> readable while the original site is down. There is no advertising on
> it and nothing is for sale. It is just an archive.
>
> I came across your photos on notrehistoire.ch, the Paillard assembly
> line at Yverdon and the Bolex repair workshop. They are the only
> pictures I have found anywhere of these cameras actually being built
> and repaired, which is a real gap in what is online about Bolex.
>
> Would you be happy for me to use a few of them on the site? I would
> credit the Musée d'Yverdon et région on every photo and link back to
> notrehistoire.ch. If you would rather I used only one or two, or
> smaller versions, that is completely fine, and if the answer is no
> that is fine too.
>
> Thanks very much,
> Andrew

## 6. Bolex International SA

bolex.com states that all pictures on it are the property of Bolex
International SA and may not be reproduced without written permission,
so nothing from there is used. They still trade from Sainte-Croix.

Worth asking, but expect silence. A company has no particular reason to
answer, and the material we most want (the early factory, Moïse
Paillard, Bogopolsky) may not be theirs to give anyway.

> Subject: A few old photos for a Bolex archive
>
> Hello,
>
> I run bolexcollector.org. It is a preservation site for
> Paillard-Bolex cameras and projectors, and it keeps Michael Tisdale's
> BolexCollector.com readable while the original is offline. There is
> no advertising on it and nothing is for sale.
>
> The part I am stuck on is the early history. There is very little
> online about the first decades, and the historical photos on your
> company page are the best I have seen. I noticed the site says they
> cannot be reproduced without permission, so I have not used any.
>
> Would you consider letting me use a few of them? I am thinking of
> the early factory and the founders rather than anything current. I
> would credit Bolex International SA on each one and link to your
> site. Even two or three would make a real difference to the
> timeline.
>
> Happy to send a link to what I have built so far if that helps.
>
> Thanks for your time,
> Andrew

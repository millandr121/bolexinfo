I would like to introduce myself. I'm a Bolex collector and user in the United States, in the state of Texas in fact. I've been shooting film on a casual basis for over 30 years. One of my first Bolexes was a regular 8mm B8 model. I later acquired a P2 which I still have today. When I moved up to 16mm my third 16mm camera was an early Bolex H16 (the first was a B&H 70 and the second a B&H 240). My 16mm collection includes several Bell & Howells, Kodaks, a Pathe and three Bolexes: an M4, an M5 and a Rex5. MY 8mm collection includes several Japanese cameras (Yashica, Sankyo, etc.), a Russian Quartz, Canons and Bell & Howells. I also have a few 35mm movie cameras: a DeVry type newsreel camera, and two Russian models; a Konvas and a Rodina. As for 8mm Bolexes I have two H8s, the P2 and a recently purchased S1.

I'm on a lot of film making and animation forums. Though most of my online time right now is taken up with maintaining my deviant Art gallery:

http://hmontes.deviantart.com/

So you're in Atlanta? I know someone in Atlanta. He's a freelance cartoonist for DC Comics. A lot of my equipment comes from various sources. There is the semi-annual Houston Photographic Collectors show where I find a number of movie cameras and lenses. The local flea markets don't yield very much though I did recently find a rare 16mm Kodak Analyst projector there. I used to lurk around the pawn shops of Houston looking for film gear but that was many years ago. Lately it has been eBay and a few online camera dealers. Mostly Igor's (I got the S1 from him), Ritz Camera, Pacific Rim Camera (I found the 35mm camera on their site), and Raf Camera where I got my Russian cameras from.

About the National Cine Camera. From one post at the Cinematography forum there is a mention of the company specializing in modifications of movie equipment during the 1930s. This camera I got could have been a custom job though it is not much advanced over a lunch box DeVry. The mechanism inside is definitely DeVry. The one thing it has going for it is the rack-over focusing which is similar to a professional Mitchell except the lens racks over and not the body. Supposedly the company moved to Florida at a later time but there the trail goes cold.

I haven't shot any 35mm yet. I'm still putting together the material to make it possible. For one I had to find some 35mm short ends to shoot with. Then I needed a 35mm rewind set to break down the rolls of film (which means also finding some split reels for 35mm). Then I got some spools and cans so I break down the rolls for the National Cine camera. Finally I had to come up with a way to adapt the cores for the Rodina for the spilt reels. The cores in the Russian camera are not Kodak standard. At the same time I was moving to a bigger place since I needed room for my growing film collection. Because the Rodina is so heavy I had to acquire a special tripod to hold it (a Quikset Hercules). The gearhead the Bolex Rex5 is sitting on is a Quikset. Also I built a stop motion stage and acquired some armatures. I was planning on using clay to clad my puppets with but have also been looking into casting latex bodies. So much to do and I still want to launch a webcomic.

I've gone back to 8mm for my live filming. I just filmed the Japanfest in Houston with the last of the Kodachrome I'll ever shoot. I used a Sankyo 8-Z camera, it's a little gem of a camera. The next film I shoot will be with the Bolex S1 I ordered from Igor. I plan on using the Ektachrome 100D 8mm stock I just got. Will be curious to see how good this film stock is.

For my cartoon animation work I will use my computer. Mostly because I got the software and there are several online places to display animation especially YouTube. In fact the film I shot at the Japanfest I plan on putting on YouTube once I get the footages transferred and edited.

Cool stuff on your deviantArt gallery, Herb. Let me know when you get any shorts uploaded to YouTube; I'd love to check them out.

Yeah, Atlanta's a hotspot for animators, being the home of TBS, Cartoon Network, Turner South... and everything else related to Turner Broadcasting. One local animator (well, he recently moved to Wyoming) has a blog I like to read: Ward-O-Matic -- I rarely read blogs, but I like his animation style.

You're lucky to have a photo collector show down there. I haven't seen one in my area since the mid '90s. I don't find much quality stuff from flea markets and thrift stores, but at least I find cheap stuff. Estate sales seem to be the best source for Bolex and other good cameras, at least for me; it's nice being able to handle the camera and visually inspect it first, rather than trust online pictures from an auction. I buy on eBay also, but I always bid as if I were buying a broken camera -- despite what the seller's description claims. It seems there's almost always something missing or defective, or it doesn't run as well as described.

I hope to shoot some 100D this summer in Las Vegas. I wish I could bring my H8, but I'll probably use a D8L or other pocket size camera. If I have enough room in my camera bag or luggage, I'd like to take the S1.

By the way, I have a depth of field chart for the Variogon zoom on the S1. (My S1 included everything but the operating instructions for the camera.) I think I recall you mentioning not having a manual in the Bolex thread at filmshooting.com. If your camera didn't come with the Variogon DOF chart, I'll scan a copy for you if you'd like. In the meantime, if you know anyone who's selling an S1 instruction manual for cheap, let me know.

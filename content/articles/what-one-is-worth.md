This archive can tell you what Paillard charged for a thing when it was new, and it cannot tell you what yours is worth now. Nobody can, from a page: condition decides most of it, and there is no public record of what this equipment actually trades for. What follows is how to find out for yourself, in the order the answers are worth having.

## 1. Read sold listings, never asking prices

This is the single most important habit, and almost nobody does it. eBay is full of Bolex gear priced far above anything that ever sells, sitting unsold for years and relisted forever. Those numbers are what a hopeful seller wants, not what anyone paid, and averaging them will tell you your camera is worth two or three times what it is.

On eBay, the filter you want is buried but free:

1. Search for the model, then open **Filter** or scroll the left-hand sidebar.
2. Tick **Sold items**. Completed items shows unsold ones too, which is not what you want.
3. Sort by most recent, and ignore anything that is obviously a different model, a lot of mixed gear, or a parts camera unless that is what you have.

That gives roughly the last ninety days, and it is the whole of what anyone can see without an account.

If you sell on eBay, there is a much better tool and it is free. Do not go looking for Terapeak: it was a separate company and a separate site, eBay bought it, and there is nothing left to visit. It now lives inside your own account, at **Seller Hub, then Research, then Product research**. It reaches back about three years instead of ninety days, which for equipment that sells a few times a month is the difference between a figure and a guess.

The catch is that eBay gates it behind selling. Their help pages say you need at least one active listing, or to have sold something in the last ninety days, and the pages disagree with each other about whether every seller gets it or only Store subscribers. So it is worth a look if you list anything at all, and not worth opening an account for on its own. For everyone else the Sold items filter above is the tool, and three years of history is simply not available.

## 2. Auction results are the fairest figures

An auction price is what two or more people who both wanted the thing were willing to pay on the day, with no seller's wishful thinking in it. That makes auction results the closest thing to a true market value this equipment has. They run lower than a dealer's price and higher than a car-boot find, and if you only look at one kind of number, look at these. Camera-specialist auction houses publish past results, and eBay listings that ran as real auctions rather than fixed price are the same thing in miniature.

## 3. Ask the people who actually trade them

After sold listings and auctions, the collectors themselves are the best source, and for some things they are the only one. A great deal of Bolex equipment never touches eBay at all: it changes hands inside the user groups, between people who know what they are looking at.

Those groups also keep prices honest. Post something at a silly number and somebody will say so, kindly or otherwise, and post something underpriced and somebody will tell you that too. It is a market with its own referees, which is more than can be said for a search results page.

Where collectors gather is listed on the [links page](/links).

## 4. Marketplace is luck, and where the bargains are

Facebook Marketplace and the local classifieds are the worst place to find a price and the best place to find a deal. Sellers there are usually clearing a house rather than trading cameras, so a working H-16 can appear at a tenth of what it would fetch among collectors, and equally a seized one can be listed at the price of a serviced one. There is no record of what sold, no history, and no way to research it. Location decides everything: you are limited to what happens to surface near you, and this equipment is far too heavy and too fragile to be worth shipping on a chance. Worth watching patiently. Not worth valuing from.

## Reading the number once you have it

**Condition is most of the price.** A recently serviced body and an untested one are the same model and nowhere near the same money. A camera that has not run in twenty years will want a service costing a meaningful fraction of what the camera is worth, and that cost comes off the price, not out of the buyer's goodwill.

**What is attached matters as much.** The glass is often worth more than the body it is on. A turret with three good Switars is a different object from the same camera bare, and a rare lens can carry an otherwise ordinary outfit.

**Three sales is not a market.** This gear sells rarely enough that any figure you arrive at is a range, not a price. Treat the middle of several recent sold results as the honest answer and the extremes as noise.

## Help build the record

None of the above exists as a dataset anyone can consult, which is why this is a method and not a table. So the archive is building one: every item page takes what readers paid, with the condition and where it was bought, and shows a figure once enough reports agree. Nothing is published until it has been read, and nothing is shown from a single sale.

If you have bought or sold anything Bolex, adding it is the most useful minute you can give this site. Find the item and use the line at the foot of its page, or [tell us here](/suggest).

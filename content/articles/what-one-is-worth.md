Film came back, and Bolexes came back with it. A new generation has picked these cameras up and started shooting with them again, which is the best thing that has happened to them in forty years. It also means they cost more than they used to, and the question we get asked most is some version of *what is mine worth*. The honest answer is a method rather than a number, because a Bolex sells for whatever a buyer is willing to pay for it.

Start with sold listings, never asking prices. eBay is full of Bolex gear sitting at optimistic numbers, listed and relisted for months without selling. Nothing is wrong with trying your luck, but a hope is not a sale, and averaging hopes will tell you your camera is worth two or three times what it is. The filter you want is free and slightly buried: search the model, open **Filter** or the left-hand sidebar, and tick **Sold items** rather than Completed items, which includes everything that never sold. That gives you roughly ninety days of real transactions, and they usually sit well below the unsold ones. The trade-off is patience, because a seller priced at the market is rarer than one priced at the dream. If you sell on eBay yourself, Seller Hub has a research tool under **Research → Product research** reaching back about three years instead of ninety days.

Auctions land close to fair. An auction price is what two people who both wanted the thing were prepared to pay on the day, which is about as near a true market value as this equipment gets, and they have become a genuinely good way to buy and sell at a sensible number. Facebook Marketplace is the other place to watch: it has been getting livelier every year and is now a real hot spot for Bolexes. The catch is that it is intensely local, so the same camera is cheap in one city and dear in another depending on who happens to be clearing a cupboard near you. Brilliant for a patient local buyer, close to useless as a guide to what anything is worth.

Which is where the user groups come in, and for a lot of this gear they are the best place of all. The Bolex group on Facebook and the cinematography communities are full of people who have been doing this for years, and equipment changes hands there constantly. The difference is the feedback: post something at a silly number and somebody will gently say so, post something underpriced and somebody will tell you that too, and between them buyers and sellers get nudged towards what things are actually trading for this month. It is a market with its own referees, which is more than a search results page can offer. Start from the [links page](/links).

Once you have a number, remember that condition is most of the price. A recently serviced body and an untested one are the same model and nowhere near the same money, and this was true when they were new: Paillard's own service centres charged $48.00 to overhaul an H camera when the camera itself was around $300.00. The glass matters as much, and often more than the body it is attached to. And three sales is not a market, so take the middle of several recent sold results and treat the extremes as noise.

The days of a reflex Bolex for a few hundred are behind us, and there is no use pretending otherwise. But patient people still find good ones at fair money every week, usually by knowing what fair money is before they start looking. That is the whole trick, and it is a happy problem to have: these cameras are being used again rather than sitting in cupboards.
